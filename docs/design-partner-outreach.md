# Design Partner Outreach

## Target Account Criteria

Prioritize companies that meet most of these conditions:

- 200-2,000 employees
- hiring for product, engineering, design, data, or GTM knowledge-work roles
- already using Greenhouse or Ashby
- strong inbound applicant volume
- likely to attract AI-assisted or agent-assisted applicants
- modern enough to try a narrow workflow pilot without a full enterprise procurement cycle

## Priority Segments

Start with segments where applicant volume and AI-forward behavior are both likely:

- AI tooling and developer infrastructure companies
- product-led B2B SaaS companies
- high-growth startups with visible hiring brands
- recruiting-tech-adjacent companies that understand the pain early

Avoid giant enterprise-first targets at the beginning. The goal is fast feedback, not maximum logo size.

## Buyer and Champion Titles

Buyer titles:

- Head of Talent
- VP of Talent
- Recruiting Operations Lead
- Talent Systems Lead

Champion titles:

- Senior Recruiter
- Recruiting Manager
- Talent Programs Lead
- Recruiting Coordinator with ops influence

For smaller companies, the hiring founder or COO can be the buyer and champion at the same time.

## Outreach Sequence

Use a short, founder-led sequence:

1. warm intro if available
2. short cold email with one concrete pain point and a clear ask
3. LinkedIn note if the email gets no response
4. one follow-up message with the repo and example flow
5. stop after that unless interest appears

The ask is not "buy our recruiting platform." It is "help us shape a managed intake service for agent-submitted applications."

## Starter Target List

Build a fresh ten-account working list immediately before each outreach cycle. Job listings turn over quickly, so company names and role URLs do not belong in this evergreen playbook.

For each candidate account, record:

- the live role URL and the date it was revalidated
- the ATS in use
- the specific hiring-system trigger in the role description
- the likely buyer and champion
- why agent-submitted applications would matter to that team now
- a single sentence of evidence that can be cited in outreach

Revalidate every listing on the day a message is sent. Remove an account when its trigger role closes, and never imply that a company has a current hiring need based on an archived listing.

## Sample Cold Email

Subject: official channel for AI applicants

Hi {{FirstName}},

I have a hunch talent teams are about to get buried by AI-assisted applications coming through forms that were never built for them.

We're building Apply via Agent, a managed intake layer that sits in front of the ATS, translates reusable applicant data into the employer's schema, and adds consent, provenance, and duplicate checks before anything hits recruiter workflow.

We're looking for a few design partners already feeling this pain. If this is on your radar, I'd love to show you the repo and compare notes on what a pilot on a handful of live roles would need to look like.

Worth a quick conversation next week?

Best,
Andre

## Customized Outreach Drafts

Use the draft that matches the account's verified trigger, then replace every generic phrase with evidence from the live role or company hiring page.

### Building a Hiring System

Subject: hiring engine

Hi,

I saw you're building the recruiting system from the ground up. That feels like exactly the moment when teams can choose whether candidate AI comes through the same old human forms or through a cleaner machine channel.

We're building Apply via Agent, a managed intake layer that sits in front of the ATS, translates reusable applicant data into the employer's schema, and adds consent, provenance, and duplicate checks before anything lands in recruiter workflow.

Feels like the kind of system decision that's much easier to make early than after inbound gets messy. If this is relevant, I'd love to compare notes on what a pilot on a few engineering roles would need to look like.

Best,
Andre

Subject: recruiting stack

Hi,

I saw your team is defining its recruiting stack, including the ATS and interview systems.

I'm working on something adjacent: a managed intake layer for agent-submitted applications. The idea is to let employers accept structured machine-readable submissions, translate reusable applicant data into the ATS schema, and keep duplicate or low-trust noise out of recruiter workflow.

It struck me as the kind of thing that is easiest to evaluate while the stack is still being defined. If useful, I can send over the repo and example flow.

Best,
Andre
