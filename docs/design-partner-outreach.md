# Design Partner Outreach

## Target Account Criteria

Prioritize companies that meet most of these conditions:

- 200-2,000 employees
- hiring for product, engineering, design, data, or GTM knowledge-work roles
- already using Greenhouse or Ashby
- strong inbound applicant volume
- likely to attract AI-assisted or agent-assisted applicants
- modern enough to try a narrow workflow pilot without a full enterprise procurement cycle

## Priority Segments

Start with segments where applicant volume and AI-forward behavior are both likely:

- AI tooling and developer infrastructure companies
- product-led B2B SaaS companies
- high-growth startups with visible hiring brands
- recruiting-tech-adjacent companies that understand the pain early

Avoid giant enterprise-first targets at the beginning. The goal is fast feedback, not maximum logo size.

## Buyer and Champion Titles

Buyer titles:

- Head of Talent
- VP of Talent
- Recruiting Operations Lead
- Talent Systems Lead

Champion titles:

- Senior Recruiter
- Recruiting Manager
- Talent Programs Lead
- Recruiting Coordinator with ops influence

For smaller companies, the hiring founder or COO can be the buyer and champion at the same time.

## Outreach Sequence

Use a short, founder-led sequence:

1. warm intro if available
2. short cold email with one concrete pain point and a clear ask
3. LinkedIn note if the email gets no response
4. one follow-up message with the repo and example flow
5. stop after that unless interest appears

The ask is not "buy our recruiting platform." It is "help us shape a managed intake service for agent-submitted applications."

## Sample Cold Email

Subject: official channel for AI applicants

Hi {{FirstName}},

I have a hunch talent teams are about to get buried by AI-assisted applications coming through forms that were never built for them.

We're building Apply via Agent, a managed intake layer that sits in front of the ATS, translates reusable applicant data into the employer's schema, and adds consent, provenance, and duplicate checks before anything hits recruiter workflow.

We're looking for a few design partners already feeling this pain. If this is on your radar, I'd love to show you the repo and compare notes on what a pilot on a handful of live roles would need to look like.

Worth a quick conversation next week?

Best,
Andre

## Sample LinkedIn Note

Working on something for talent teams dealing with AI-assisted applicants. The idea is to give employers a managed intake path that translates reusable applicant data into ATS-ready submissions instead of forcing everything through human forms. If that pain is showing up on your side, I'd love to compare notes.

## Sample Follow-Up Email

Subject: hiring intake

Hi {{FirstName}},

Following up because I think this gets real very quickly once candidate agents become normal behavior.

The wedge for us is not "help people auto-apply." It's giving employers a managed intake layer that can accept structured submissions, map them into the ATS, and keep duplicate or low-trust noise out of recruiter workflow.

If this is worth a look, I can send over the repo and the example flow first.

Best,
Andre
